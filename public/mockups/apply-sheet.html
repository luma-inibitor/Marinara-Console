Apply sheet & decision rail

The rail keeps track of how far through you are. The sheet handles everything about applying. Apply is the door between them.

shown at phone width · 486px · real fonts and icons · big number at 24px

§1 What the rail keeps

One decision leaks in here from the copy pass: the bar button now reads Review and apply rather than Apply, so the two Apply buttons are not both called Apply. That costs 81px in this row. It may not be needed any more — the panel's own button now reads "Apply 9 changes", which already tells them apart. Reverting it to "Apply ›" is a one-word change if you want the width back.

Four things leave the rail: the number inside the Apply button, the warning badge, the "2 drafts will be applied" line, and the counts being tappable. All of that is now behind Apply. What is left is how far through you are, whether your work is saved, and the two buttons you press while working. Three ways to lay that out.

Switching the save badge to an icon (§5) claws back 79px of that, which is not enough on its own.

W2 and W3 are icon-only in all three states. W4 keeps an icon for the two states you can ignore and gives the one you cannot a visible way out. Note what happens to W2 at this size: the upload cloud and the tick cloud are nearly the same shape, and "storing" versus "stored" is exactly the pair you need to tell apart at a glance. W3 sidesteps it by using one mark at two strengths instead of two marks.

Tap on a phone, hover or keyboard-focus on a desktop. This is the same tooltip the memory types and claim kinds already use, so it is one thing to learn rather than a new one.

Measured: the badge goes from 53px to 33px when stored, and from 112px to 33px when it could not store. W4 keeps a visible Retry and lands at 89px. Since the failed badge is what pushed the single-row layout 30px over the edge on this phone, W2 and W3 rescue it and W4 does not.

the problem an icon makes worse

Saved

A blind copy review flagged that the word "Saved" already fights the promise that nothing is written until you press Apply — read plainly, it says your changes are in. A tick makes that worse, because a tick is the most finished-looking mark there is, and the cloud-with-a-tick is a tick plus "sent somewhere". The disk is the only one of the three that says stored without also saying done.

§4 What I would pick

R3 and S1. R1 is ruled out by measurement — it fits your phone with 107px to spare and then loses the Apply button by 30px on a 390px one. Between the two survivors I would take R3: the progress bar is the thing you glance at most often and right now it is a 4px hairline nobody notices, and moving it to the top of the rail costs no height at all because the counts become its caption. S1 over S2 because the bar has to be drawn in greys to stay inside our colour rules, and telling three greys apart is the one thing a bar is bad at.

Open question I could not settle on my own: the "which memories" list is sorted biggest first. That means a brand-new memory getting one write sorts below an existing one getting three, even though creating something new is the bigger deal. Do you want new memories pulled to the top of that list regardless of size?
